The trust gap

Trust breaks on both sides.

Before a project begins, the signal is scattered. After it ends, the proof is often scattered too.

Clientsbuyer side
  • Hard to verify reliabilityReferences and reviews rarely show the full delivery record.
  • No consistent track recordEvery marketplace keeps its own partial version of the story.
  • Gut feel fills the gapsUnclear scope and acceptance criteria create avoidable uncertainty.
  • Risk slows decisionsMore perceived risk means slower hiring and more defensive process.
Service providersseller side
  • Hard to prove completed workEvidence is spread across chats, drives, invoices, and platforms.
  • Scope moves quietlyDates, revisions, and expectations can change without a shared version.
  • Payment terms stay uncertainGood work does not always create a clean record of what was agreed.
  • Recourse is unclearWhen expectations diverge, the trail is incomplete for both parties.

What if trust could be demonstrated instead of assumed?

The answer is not a universal score. It is a clearer record: identity, commitment, evidence, outcome, and both sides’ response.

The scale of the context

The cost of uncertainty compounds.

The wider delayed-payment context makes the trust gap more than an abstract problem. These figures are context for the economic friction around commitments and payments — not Deadline’s market size, revenue, recoveries, or proof that one platform solves the whole issue.

Official-source figures as presented in the founder briefing: Economic Survey 2025–26 and Ministry of MSME / MSME Samadhaan reporting.
